The 15-Minute Helsinki
The 15-Minute Helsinki · 1 of 5

Only the bright core has it all on foot.

A "15-minute city" means daily life — work, shops, care, learning, somewhere to enjoy — within a 15-minute walk. By that test only Helsinki’s core qualifies: just 1,604 of 6,656 cells score a full five on foot.

Press bike · 2 of 5

…and the city breathes open.

The complete area more than doubles — 1,604 → 3,603 cells. A bike adds no roads; it just makes the same Helsinki reachable.

Step outside the core · 3 of 5

Almost everything falls out of reach at once.

Past the centre a 15-minute walk stops covering daily life — 5,052 of 6,656 cells miss at least one need. The two that tip first are work (2,091) and care (1,929), neck and neck. Colours show the first thing too far.

This is walking proximity, not provision — Helsinki has clinics and offices; outside the core they’re just past a 15-minute walk.

    How this works
    • It’s a count, not an average. The number is how many of the five needs (working, supplying, caring, learning, enjoying) are reachable.
    • "Living" isn’t scored — home is the trip origin.
    • Travel is cell-to-cell, not door-to-door — a precomputed lookup between 250 m cell centres; fast but coarse.
    • The reach ring is approximate — it suggests how far ~15 min gets you; it is not a routed isochrone.
    • Mode = a specific variant: Walk (average), Bike (fast), Transit (rush-hour), Car (rush-hour).
